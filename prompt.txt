PART A: CONTROLS AND UI FIXES
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. SMART DIG MODE (TAB TOGGLE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Today digging always targets the cursor (`crates/game/src/run/input.rs` and
`run/player.rs`). Holding dig while moving often snags the player on single-pixel
bits of terrain.

Add a second dig mode and toggle between them with Tab:
  - Cursor dig (the current behaviour): dig where the cursor points.
  - Smart dig (like Terraria's smart cursor): dig the nearest cells that block the
    player. With dig held, it picks the cells closest to the player's body in the
    direction the player is moving or aiming (the movement keys take priority; with
    no movement keys held, use the direction toward the cursor). It clears whatever
    would stop the player's collision box from moving that way, including small
    lips and single pixels at foot and head height. Holding dig plus a direction
    should let the player tunnel smoothly in that direction without getting caught.
    Holding down should dig straight down beneath the player.
  - In smart dig mode, highlight the cells that will be dug next with a clear,
    readable outline or tint that works against every material and in darkness.
  - Show the current mode on the HUD (a small icon plus label), play a short sound
    when it changes, and remember the choice in the save file.
  - Dig speed, hardness rules, undiggable materials and item effects on digging
    (e.g. what Crumbling Pick does today) must work the same in both modes.
  - Update the autoplay bot so it still works, and use smart dig in it if that helps.

PART B: THE WIZARD REWORK (WIZARD FANTASY HORROR CAVE GAME)
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE NEW PREMISE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The game becomes a wizard fantasy horror cave game. You play an apprentice
elemental wizard who must descend to the core of the earth to complete their
wizard training. Along the way they collect spell scrolls that grant elemental
magic. Many apprentices went down before them, and most never came back.

The tone is mystical dark fantasy with horror: dread, isolation, darkness, things
moving just outside your light, and the remains and journals of apprentices who
failed. Aim for unsettling and atmospheric rather than gory.

Rename the game if a better title fits the new theme, and update the menu, window
title and README to match. Keep the code's existing structure and module names
unless renaming clearly helps.

What stays the same: the falling-sand simulation, the layered descent, permadeath,
random seeds, achievements unlocking new content for later runs, and the most
important design rule. Every upgrade must change how the player interacts with
materials rather than adjusting numbers.

Design all new run state so it could be synced for multiplayer later (plain data,
host-authoritative-friendly), as the existing code already does, and update the
README's multiplayer section to match.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. CORE CHANGES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The wizard's staff replaces every tool:
  - Dig spell: digging is now a spell cast from the staff, aimed and held as
    before (both dig modes from section 1). Give it a visible beam or bolt effect
    and casting sounds. Its speed still depends on material hardness. The old pick
    items become dig-spell variants in the new schools (e.g. Crumbling Pick turns
    into a terramancy scroll).
  - Light orb spell replaces torches: cast a floating ball of light that sticks
    where it lands or drifts slowly, lights the area and fades over time. Limit it
    with charges or a cooldown instead of a torch count. Decide whether it ignites
    flammables (a pyromancy upgrade could add that).
  - Rope is removed from the game completely: the material, the input, the HUD
    counter, the art, the sound, the loadouts and any achievement that depends on
    it. The player still needs to be able to climb out of holes, so make sure
    the base movement (wall climb, wall jump, and digging steps with the dig
    spell) plus schools like aeromancy or terramancy cover that. Playtest that a
    player without any upgrades can't get permanently stuck.
  - Ore is replaced by collectable crystals: glowing crystal shards that drop from
    crystal veins and from chests. They are the currency for shrines. There is
    already a Crystal material in the sim; make the collectable currency clearly
    distinct from it in name and look, or merge the two deliberately.
  - Loadouts become wizard starting choices (see section 6).

Mana: decide whether spells use a shared mana bar, per-spell charges and
cooldowns, or both. Pick whatever creates the most interesting decisions and keep
the HUD readable. Document the choice in the plan.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. ELEMENTAL SCHOOLS AND THE TWO-SCHOOL SPECIALIZATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

There is a wide range of elemental schools, but every wizard specializes in
exactly two of them per run. Every pair of schools must synergize in its own,
fun way, so the choice of two defines that run's build.

Schools (around 8; adjust names and the list as the design needs, but keep each
tied to materials and interactions in the simulation):
  - Pyromancy (fire, heat, lava, embers)
  - Hydromancy (water, steam, currents, flooding)
  - Terramancy (stone, sand, gravel, shaping and collapsing terrain)
  - Cryomancy (ice, frost, freezing liquids, cold)
  - Aeromancy (wind, gas, pushing powders and liquids, levitation)
  - Fulmancy (lightning, sparks, conduction through water and metal)
  - Alchemy or Venomancy (acid, poison, corrosion, transmutation)
  - Mycomancy (fungus, growth, spores, rot)
  Optional: Lumomancy (light, crystal) or Umbramancy (shadow, darkness), which
  fit the horror theme well.

Specialization rules (make the first version work like this, then tune it):
  - At the start of a run the wizard chooses one school from the unlocked ones
    (their first attunement).
  - The second attunement is chosen from the first altar in layer 1, which offers
    scrolls from a few other schools. Choosing one locks in the pair.
  - From then on, scrolls come only from the wizard's two schools, plus a small
    pool of neutral utility scrolls. If a scroll from another school ever appears
    (e.g. on a fallen apprentice), it can be broken down into crystals instead of
    learned.
  - Each pair of schools unlocks a fusion: a named spell or passive that only exists
    for that pair, appearing once the wizard has learned at least one scroll of
    each school. For 8 schools that is 28 fusions. Each must feel distinct and
    should come mostly from the simulation (e.g. Hydromancy + Fulmancy: an
    electrified flood; Pyromancy + Cryomancy: thermal shock that shatters stone;
    Terramancy + Aeromancy: sandstorms that bury creatures; Mycomancy + Pyromancy:
    explosive spore clouds).
  - Write a table of all schools, their scrolls, and all 28 fusions in the plan
    before implementing them.

Scrolls:
  - At least 4 scrolls per school (at least 32 in total) plus a few neutral ones.
    Each has a name, icon, short description and flavour line, and changes how the
    wizard interacts with materials. Reuse the existing 17 items wherever they fit
    a school (Water Canister -> hydromancy, Frost Seed -> cryomancy, Spark Rod ->
    fulmancy, Fungal Spores -> mycomancy, Acid Flask -> alchemy, Magma Pick ->
    pyromancy, and so on).
  - Scrolls are found on altars and in chests and bought at shrines with crystals,
    as items are today.

Meta progression:
  - Start with 3-4 schools unlocked. Unlock the others through achievements that
    teach that school's materials (e.g. survive a flood -> Hydromancy).
  - Achievements can also unlock individual scrolls and wizard starting choices.
    Rework the existing 13 achievements to fit, remove the rope-related ones, and
    add new ones so there are at least 20.
  - The Unlocks & Achievements screen shows schools, scrolls and fusions
    (discovered fusions are revealed, undiscovered ones are hidden with a hint).
  - Old save files must load safely: map old items and achievements to their new
    equivalents where possible, otherwise drop them, and never crash.

7. WORLD, CREATURES AND HORROR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The world keeps its layers and set pieces, re-themed to mystical dark fantasy:
  - Rename layers to fit (e.g. the Whispering Crust, the Drowned Halls, the
    Fungal Abyss, the Molten Sanctum, the Heart of the World). The core becomes the
    final trial of the wizard's training. Touching it completes the training and
    wins the run.
  - Add environmental storytelling: remains of earlier apprentices with dropped
    scrolls or crystals, journal fragments (short, readable lines that build the
    lore and dread), ruined shrines, ritual circles, sealed doors.

Creatures get fantasy horror designs and must keep interacting with the
simulation (burn, drown, freeze, get crushed, get dissolved). For example:
  - Hollowed apprentices: failed wizards who still cast twisted spells of a school.
  - Things that hunt by light: they're drawn to the light orb and your glow, so
    darkness becomes a real choice.
  - Mimic chests, blind cave wyrms that burrow through terrain, spore-puppets in
    the fungal layer, and molten things in the deep.
  - At least one creature per layer, and one rare, frightening encounter (an
    unkillable stalker or a lurker that can only be escaped or trapped using the
    simulation).

Horror atmosphere: darker lighting, flickering light orbs, subtle visual effects
(vignette, faint distortion near certain creatures), occasional unexplained
sounds, and moments of silence.
