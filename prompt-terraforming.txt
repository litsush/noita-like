================================================================================
1. THE GAME
================================================================================

PREMISE: You are terraformers sent from Earth to a similar planet in a nearby
galaxy to prepare it for human habitation.

GENRE: A blend of a Terraria-style sandbox and automation/idle games like Cookie
Clicker. Early on, everything is done by hand. Over time, players build up
plants, domes, pipes, generators and robots that work for them, and numbers keep
climbing even while the players are off exploring.

GAMEPLAY LOOP:
  mine underground -> sell mined resources -> buy cybernetic body upgrades and
  equipment/resources for the base -> repeat, until the base produces enough
  atmospheric oxygen, food, fresh water and living space for humanity to come.

WIN CONDITION: Have enough Apartment Domes, a high enough atmospheric oxygen
level, and enough food and fresh water output to support the cargo ship full of
humans. Show progress toward every target clearly at all times (a Colony Readiness
panel). When all targets are met, the players can call the ship from the Comm.
Terminal, and it lands in a memorable ending sequence. Afterwards, the world stays
playable.

AESTHETIC: Plant punk. Similar vibes to Rain World (overgrown industrial ruins,
moody atmospheric lighting, a living alien ecosystem, rain and mist), but fully
pixel art and with far more plants. Lush, strange alien flora growing over cold
human machinery. Animated foliage that sways and reacts to players and wind.

START OF GAME: Players spawn in a starter biodome. The air inside is breathable.
It has 8 beds, two storage chests, and a rack that holds exploration suits, since
the atmosphere doesn't have enough oxygen to breathe at the start.

================================================================================
2. MULTIPLAYER (STEAM CO-OP, UP TO 8 PLAYERS)
================================================================================

  - Up to 8 players can join the same world over Steam (and over LAN with the
    existing `--host-lan` / `--join-lan` flags, for testing without Steam).
  - The host is authoritative. Everything in this prompt must stay in sync for all
    players: terrain, plants and their growth, domes, pipes and water, machines,
    robots and their programs, inventories, chests, credits, atmosphere, creatures,
    day/night and weather.
  - The world is persistent: the host saves it and can load it again later. Players
    can join and leave at any time, and their character, inventory and body mods are
    kept for when they rejoin.
  - Credits are shared by the colony. Inventories and body mods are per player.
  - Each player needs a way to tell apart (name tags and a colour accent on their
    suit) and a map marker for every teammate.

================================================================================
3. WORLD, SURVIVAL AND OXYGEN
================================================================================

  - A large 2D world with a surface (lakes, alien plant life, ruins), and
    contiguous underground cave networks similar to Terraria, with entrances on the
    surface. Generate it deterministically from a seed.
  - A day/night cycle (battery flowers grow each day) and weather (rain, mist) that
    supports the aesthetic and the water systems.
  - Exploration suits: outside breathable air, players use a suit with a limited
    oxygen supply that refills inside domes or near oxygen sources. The suit is shown
    on the sprite. As the planet's atmosphere improves, suits drain more slowly, and
    at a high enough level players can breathe outside without one.

OXYGEN: The planet's oxygen level isn't high enough to sustain humans yet. Biodomes
produce excess oxygen that is released into the atmosphere. The more plants you
have, the higher the atmosphere's oxygen level gets, slowly but surely. Later in
the game there are other ways to raise oxygen production. With iron, silicon and
power from battery flowers, players can synthesize Oxygen Generators to place
outside. Show the global oxygen level and its rate of change on the HUD.

CAVES AND ORES: Inside the caves are dangerous cave predators (creepy things like
alien spiders) but also precious minerals and ores (iron, copper, gold, silicon
and rarer ones deeper down) used in the synthesizer to create upgrades and
equipment. Deeper caves are darker, more dangerous and richer.

================================================================================
4. POWER: BATTERY FLOWERS
================================================================================

Battery flowers grow each day on the planet's surface and must be harvested to
power equipment. They can also be grown in Green Domes. Harvested battery flowers
are an item that charges machines, robots and equipment. Design how power is
stored and delivered (e.g. carrying flowers to machines, charging stations, or a
power network) so that it supports automation and stays readable.

================================================================================
5. THE LASER
================================================================================

By default, players can shoot a simple laser bolt that consumes battery. The
battery recharges on its own with no resources needed, but takes extra time to
recharge if it is fully depleted, so players should be careful not to overuse
power. Show the battery meter on the HUD.

================================================================================
6. THE SYNTHESIZER, EARTH AND CREDITS
================================================================================

THE SYNTHESIZER turns materials into equipment, machines, domes, robots and body
mods using recipes (blueprints). Players start with a set of basic recipes.

EARTH: Players can sell excess minerals to Earth at the Comm. Terminal in exchange
for credits. Credits buy animals, seeds, human workers, and blueprints for new
synthesizer recipes. Decide what human workers do (e.g. they live in Bedroom or
Apartment domes and boost a dome's output) and document it.

================================================================================
7. DOMES, FARMING, WATER AND PLANTS
================================================================================

DOMES: Domes are synthesized and then placed in the world. A dome can't touch
another dome. A placed dome forms a breathable interior around the player.
Dome types:
  - Green Dome: needs lots of glass, iron, dirt, fertilizer and wood. Contains 4
    planters for growing all sorts of crops, including battery flowers.
  - Storage Dome: bulk storage.
  - Bedroom Dome: housing for players and human workers.
  - Kitchen Dome: turns crops into food.
  - Dark Dome: for growing mushrooms and other darkness-related tasks.
  - Dome Dome: slowly builds other domes without resources; it just takes time.
  - Aqua Dome: for growing aquatic plants, farming marine life, etc.
  - Apartment Dome: housing for the incoming colonists. A certain number is
    required to win.
  - Barn Dome: farm animals not included; they must be requested from Earth.
  - Water Generator Dome: produces water (see below).
Each dome needs a clear interior layout, its own look, and a short tooltip
explaining what it does and what it needs.

WATER DISTRIBUTION: Water can be sourced from lakes on the planet or produced with
Water Generator Domes. Players build copper pipe networks to automate water for:
drinking water, sprinkler systems inside Green Domes, and coolant systems for other
machinery. Pipes show flow visually, and there is an overlay that shows networks,
flow rates and problems.

PLANT GENETICS: After saving up enough credits, players can buy a seed gene
splicer, which splices the genes of any two plant seeds to create unique new
plants. Spliced plants inherit traits from both parents (e.g. oxygen output,
growth speed, yield, power output, light needs, water needs, appearance), so
splicing creates genuinely new and useful plants, not just recolours. Design
around 15-20 base species, a trait system, procedurally named and procedurally
drawn hybrids, and a codex that records every plant the colony has discovered.

FARMING: Planting, watering, fertilizing and harvesting crops, by hand at first
and automated later with sprinklers and robots.

================================================================================
8. ROBOTS AND THE SENTENCE BUILDER
================================================================================

Robots are synthesized from iron, silicon and gold and are powered by battery
flowers. They can be programmed to do tasks such as farming, harvesting,
scavenging in certain areas, mining, carrying items between chests, and so on.

PROGRAMMING: Robots are programmed with a sentence builder: players build plain
sentences from drop-down word blocks instead of writing code. For example:
  "WHEN [battery] is [below] [20%], [go to] [Charging Station 1]."
  "[Harvest] [battery flowers] in [Green Dome 2], then [store] them in [Storage
   Dome 1]."
  "[Mine] [iron] in [area: marked on map] UNTIL [inventory] is [full], then
   [return to] [Storage Dome 1]."
  "IF [a predator] is [nearby], [flee to] [nearest dome]."
A robot runs a short list of these sentences, with conditions, actions, targets
and areas (areas are marked by dragging a box on the map). Show what a robot is
doing and why (a speech bubble or status line), and highlight any sentence that
can't run, with a reason. Robots are synced in multiplayer and keep working while
players are elsewhere, as long as their chunks are simulated (decide how
off-screen robots and domes keep producing; an idle-game style of simulated
production for unloaded areas is fine).

9. BODY MODIFICATIONS
================================================================================

Body mods are synthesized, and Earth has loads of blueprints for new, cooler mods
too. Every mod set has three tiers that are bought in order and get progressively
cooler, plus a Legendary tier. Every tier must be visually reflected on the
player's sprite with a high-quality sprite that matches the game's art style. Make
sure all sprites are polished, not cut off, and animated when it makes sense.

Example sets (implement these):

  Rocket Feet
    Tier I    Booster Thrust: double jump, with a cool rocket burst effect from the
              feet on the second jump.
    Tier II   Rocket Hover: hold after the second jump for about 2 seconds of rocket
              thrust, for extra height and control.
    Tier III  Rocket Flight: 5 seconds of flight per jump.
    Legendary Perma-thrust: infinite flight.

  Extendo-Arms
    Tier I    Arm++: longer arms, extending your reach for every interaction.
              Animate the arm extending when you reach.
    Tier II   Arm++ Premium: an extra arm that automates nearby tasks of your
              choice. It can mine nearby ore, water nearby plants, construct
              nearby biodomes, etc. (configured with the same sentence builder as
              robots, or a simple task picker).
    Tier III  Arm++ Ultra: a second automated arm. Both arms get extra reach, and
              so do you.
    Legendary Armnipresence: a fully arm-tonomous bonus arm that flies freely and
              is controlled from the map. Open the extended map view and click a
              button to send the arm to do things anywhere on the revealed parts of
              the map. It can dig and interact with chests, farms and so on.

  Bolt Enhancements
    Tier I    Overcharge: charge your shot for a bigger, higher-damage bolt that
              uses more battery.
    Tier II   Jack Up: your bolts deal more damage and are larger.
    Tier III  Rug Pull: every shot fires two bolts, without using extra battery.
    Legendary Straight Up Scammed: bolts use 80% less energy.

Come up with 50 more mod sets and add them to the game, each with three tiers plus
a Legendary tier, names in the same playful tone, and a visible change on the
sprite at every tier. Cover a wide spread: movement, mining, combat, survival
(oxygen, cold, toxins), farming, automation, building, scanning/mapping,
logistics, and co-op support (mods that help teammates). Write all 53 sets in a
design doc before implementing them. Mods should create interesting choices, so
decide whether players can equip every mod at once or have a limited number of
body slots (head, torso, arms, legs, back, etc.), and document it.

10. INVENTORY AND QUALITY OF LIFE
================================================================================

A full Terraria-style inventory system with lots of QoL features: hotbar,
inventory grid, stack sizes, drag and drop, shift-click quick move between
inventory and chests, sorting, quick stack to nearby chests, deposit-all,
search/filter, item tooltips, trash slot, auto pickup with a magnet radius,

codex for plants, creatures and recipes. All controls must be shown and rebindable
